# Green Paper: Business Case and Monetization

The Green Paper says how the product sustains itself: who pays, for what, at what cost, how it reaches them, how anyone will know it is working, and when to stop. It inherits every White commitment and Yellow mechanism. A revenue idea that needs a White commitment broken is a White question, raised with the owner, never a quiet exception in the Green Paper.

**Derived from:** White, Yellow.

## Evidence discipline

Business papers drift into confident fiction faster than any other kind. Open with the labels the paper uses, and attach one to every claim and figure:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inherited constraint | Required by the White or Yellow Paper; not a business choice |
| Business invariant | A commercial rule the owner has fixed |
| Strategic recommendation | The paper's proposed choice, open to the owner's decision |
| Planning hypothesis | An estimate or assumption, to be tested |
| Measured result | Observed data, with its source and date |

Market sizes, conversion rates and prices are planning hypotheses until sourced or measured. Say so on the page, and do not let a hypothesis reach a pitch deck as a fact.

## Outline

Sections marked *core* appear in nearly every good Green Paper.

1. **Abstract** (core).
2. **Evidence discipline** (core). The labels above.
3. **Commercial thesis and the strongest commercial objection.** Why this can be a business, and the best case that it cannot.
4. **Inherited constraints and business principles** (core). The White and Yellow commitments the business must respect, and the commercial rules derived from them.
5. **Customers and segments** (core). Who uses, who pays (often not the same), and the order in which segments are pursued.
6. **Initial wedge and product ladder.** The first thing anyone pays for, and what follows.
7. **Free, paid and prohibited** (core). What is free and stays free, what is paid, and what the business will never charge for or sell. Make the free list explicit whenever the White Paper makes promises about access.
8. **Revenue architecture and pricing hypotheses** (core).
9. **Cost structure and unit economics** (core). Built on the Yellow Paper's scaling and cost model.
10. **Go-to-market, distribution and growth loops** (core).
11. **Competitive landscape and defensibility.** Who else serves the customer, and why this product keeps its customers. When the White Paper rules out lock-in, explain the defensibility that remains.
12. **Metrics** (core). What will be measured, the targets, and the metrics that must not be optimized because they would push against a commitment.
13. **Stage gates, kill criteria and capital allocation.** The evidence each stage needs before money and effort move to the next, and the evidence that would end the product.
14. **Risks.** A pointer to the Red Paper's register when one exists. Without a Red Paper, this section holds the non-technical risk register in the [Red Paper's format](red-paper.md#the-risk-register); when a Red Paper is written, the register moves there and this section becomes the pointer.
15. **Commercial decisions and open questions.** Decisions made, with their decision records, and questions open, each with who decides.
16. **Amendments** ([format](papers.md#versions-and-amendments)).

## What it never contains

- Technical invariants, restated or reinterpreted. Cite the Yellow Paper.
- Legal opinions. Commercial structures that raise legal or regulatory questions go into the Red Paper's counsel queue ([Counsel review](red-paper.md#counsel-review)).

## Gate to what follows

The owner accepts the Green Paper as the business basis for planning: the wedge, the free/paid/prohibited boundary and the first stage gate. Then decide with the owner whether the product needs a [Red Paper](red-paper.md#when-to-write-one); if it does not, move to the [Light Paper](light-paper.md) or straight to the [Roadmap](roadmap.md).
